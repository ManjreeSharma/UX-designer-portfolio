# 1. Takeaways
## Impact

The redesigned Identity & Access Management (IAM) experience simplified authentication, reduced task completion time, and improved overall usability. Guided workflows, streamlined navigation, and accessible interactions increased user confidence, reduced errors, and minimized dependency on IT support. The solution also provided a more consistent experience across desktop, tablet, and mobile while aligning with WCAG 2.2 AA accessibility standards.

## What I Learned

This project reinforced the importance of user-centered design, iterative usability testing, and accessibility-first thinking in enterprise applications. Collaborating with user research, identifying pain points, and validating design decisions through multiple iterations helped create a secure, intuitive, and inclusive authentication experience. Designing for accessibility from the beginning benefited all users—not just those using assistive technologies.

# 2. Next steps

## 1. Conduct Additional Usability Testing
Validate the redesigned experience with a larger and more diverse group of employees, IT administrators, managers, and users with disabilities to uncover additional usability improvements.

## 2. Expand Accessibility Compliance
Perform comprehensive WCAG 2.2 AA audits, improve screen reader support, keyboard navigation, focus management, and accessible authentication patterns across all workflows.

## 3. Launch, Measure & Iterate

Monitor analytics, task completion rates, error rates, and user feedback after implementation to continuously refine the Identity & Access Management experience through iterative design improvements.
